Tambura Oil: The Highest NPV Was Not the Best Use of Capital

A Monte Carlo reconstruction of a published, open-access capital-rationing study: three ways to power one oil-field pump, NPVs within US$0.03M of each other, capex ranging from US$0.39M to US$2.39M.

Note: the paper is open access (CC BY-NC 4.0). Its full text was read through automated extraction, not a manual PDF review. This is disclosed here and in the README.

Honest caveat: the paper does not publish its PSC split, oil price, annual cash flows, or Monte Carlo distribution shapes. This reconstruction assumes US$70/bbl, calibrates a net contractor share per option so each NPV matches the paper, uses triangular distributions on the paper's scenario ranges, and adds one correlation (oil price with inflation) the paper does not state. It does not reproduce the paper's downside: it shows far lower probability of loss than the paper's 10-30%, and its best-case upside overshoots the paper's range. Use it to see how the capital-rationing logic behaves, not as evidence about the real field.

Run your own simulation

Adjust the ranges, then run 5,000 paired trials across all three options. Oil price and inflation are drawn through a Cholesky-based Gaussian copula (Box-Muller normals, then triangular inverse-transform). Production and capex use direct inverse-transform triangular sampling. Pick an option tab to see its NPV histogram.

The lesson

NPV ranked the cable first by US$0.03M. The rental option delivered 98% of that NPV on 16% of the capital. If capital is rationed, the freed US$2.00M only has to earn US$0.03M elsewhere for the rental route to win. A board that ranks on headline NPV alone spends the scarce resource without pricing it. The paper reports no post-decision outcome, so this is a decision rule, not a result.
